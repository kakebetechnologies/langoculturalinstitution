Why this matters

The knowledge of Lango lives in people. It needs a safe home.

Clan histories, totems and the rules that go with them, proverbs, songs, the words for things the young no longer hear: much of it is carried in the memory of the elders. Lango children grow up among English at school, phones and town life. What is not passed on while the elders are with us can be lost when they are gone.

What is hard today

  • No easy way to look up Leb Lango words with their meanings on a phone, without internet.
  • Clan information is scattered, and much of what is written was written by people who are not Lango.
  • Young Lango in towns and abroad cannot easily find their clan, its totem or its story.
  • Elders, clans and institutions already hold this knowledge. They have few ways to check and correct what is written about them online.

How we began, and what we did not do

  • The starting material comes from published sources, each credited on the site: the Practical Lango Grammar and the original Lango dictionary project (the words and the phrasebook), Driberg’s The Lango (1923, out of copyright), Wikipedia, the 2024 census and the Lango Bible (read on Bible.com, not copied).
  • Clan pages carry the mark “awaiting verification” until Lango people check them. Other entries show a Verified mark only once someone has checked them.
  • We have not written Lango culture ourselves. Where there is no source yet, the app says “history being documented” instead of guessing.

The need, in one sentence

A trusted home for Lango language, clans and heritage, where Lango people decide what is true, and where every young Lango, at home or far away, can learn it.
