The need and the innovation

Culture lives in memory. We have given it a home.

The challenge

Languages, clans, songs and stories pass on by word of mouth. When elders pass away and families move to towns and abroad, the knowledge goes with them. Children learn in English, the diaspora cannot find its roots, and today’s AI tools barely understand our languages.

The innovation

Lango is a free app, website and admin system that documents, teaches and shares a people’s culture in one trusted place, in English and Leb Lango. Anyone can contribute. Language experts and clan leaders review it before anything is marked verified, and every photograph and source carries its credit.

How a contribution becomes knowledge

1
Share

A member adds a word, photo, story or clan history in the app or on the website. It is saved on the phone and sent when there is a connection.

2
Review

Language experts and clan leaders check it in the admin system and approve, correct or decline it.

3
Publish

Approved material appears in the app, the website and the open API, with its credit and its status.

4
Learn and use

Learners, schools, the diaspora and researchers use it. Points and badges reward contributors.

5
Improve

What people search for and cannot find shows where to collect next.

Trust is built in

  • Nothing is called verified until an expert says so.
  • Every photograph and source carries its credit and licence.
  • Members sign in, and the location data in photos is removed.
  • The community keeps its rights to what it shares.

From Lango to Uganda

One platform. A home for every culture.

Uganda has dozens of languages and many more clans and traditions. The Lango build separates the shared platform from the culture’s own content, so a new culture can be added without starting again.

AI, data and the ask

Data that speaks for Uganda.

Every verified word, sentence, story and recording becomes part of a dataset that belongs to the community and can train tools that understand Ugandan languages, which global AI models largely do not.
