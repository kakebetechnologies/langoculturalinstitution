# 2. The Foundation's Needs and Our Approach

*What the platform must achieve, the challenges it must overcome, and the principles behind every decision.*

The Lango live in nine districts (Lira, Apac, Oyam, Kole, Dokolo, Amolatar, Alebtong, Otuke and Kwania) and Lira City, more than two million people at the 2024 census. Leb Lango is spoken in homes, markets and on the radio; English and Kiswahili are used in offices. The Foundation carries the community's identity under the Won Nyaci, so a platform for it is not a marketing website but a **custodial** one: it holds knowledge in trust, shows where each fact came from, and leaves authority over what is published with the people the community recognises.

> **How we wrote this.** We describe the Foundation's needs from public information and from the demonstrator we have built. Points marked **[TBC]** are for the discovery workshop. We would rather ask than assume.

## Seven objectives

**Preserve** the language, clans, history and heritage in a durable, sourced archive · **Teach** words, proverbs and greetings to youth at home and abroad · **Connect** clans, districts and the diaspora · **Represent** the Foundation with an authoritative public reference · **Sustain** programmes, events and giving with visible accountability · **Include** elders and youth, town and village, online and offline · **Govern** with clear roles, review and an audit trail.

## The challenges, and how the platform answers them

| Challenge | How the platform responds |
|---|---|
| **Knowledge is held in people, not on paper.** An elder's passing can take a clan's stories with it | Guided forms for oral history, words, proverbs and sites, with voice, photo and GPS, reviewed and archived |
| **Language shift among youth** | Dictionary, English to Leb Lango translator, proverbs, greetings and lessons, usable offline on a phone |
| **A dispersed community and patchy, expensive data** | Website for reach, app for daily use, offline-first design, a diaspora corner, a clan picker on every registration |
| **Authenticity must never be in doubt** | Sources shown, trust labels, expert review, and nothing invented (Chapter 7) |
| **Paper processes and limited IT capacity** | Online forms with tracking codes, a no-code admin, role-based access, training and a support plan |
| **Sensitive public matters** | Officeholders and titles published exactly as the Foundation confirms, with an approval step; the platform takes no position |
| **Funding and accountability** | Pledges, programme progress and a transparency dashboard |

## Who the platform serves

| Audience | What they need | Where they meet the platform |
|---|---|---|
| **Youth and students** | Learn the language and history, find events, earn recognition | App, website |
| **Elders and knowledge holders** | Have their knowledge recorded and respected | Assisted contribution, admin review |
| **Clan leaders** | Maintain their clan's page, submit corrections | Clan self-service in the admin, website |
| **Diaspora** | Stay connected, learn, give, attend events | Website, app, diaspora corner |
| **Researchers, media, schools** | Citable, sourced material and open data | Website, library, open data |
| **Partners and donors** | See programmes, progress and transparency | Website, programmes and transparency pages |
| **Foundation staff** | Publish, review, report and communicate | Admin control panel |

## Guiding principles

**Community authority** (the Foundation decides what is true; the platform never invents culture) · **Offline first** (designed for the weakest connection) · **Plain language** (large touch targets, readable type, simple flows) · **Own your data** (exportable at any time) · **Built to last** (well-understood technology that local developers can maintain) · **Respect for knowledge** (consent, attribution and restricted access from the start).
