# 11. Delivery in Three Months

*Planning, design and modelling, build, content, testing and launch in 13 weeks, with a staging site the Foundation can review from week 3 and fixed gates before anything goes public.*

![fig|Figure 11.1: Three-month plan. Public launch at the end of week 13; 90 days of hypercare follow, included.](figures/fig-timeline3.svg)

| Phase | Weeks | What happens | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P0 Plan and align** | 1 to 2 | Kick-off, workshops with leadership, language experts, historians and clan leaders; content audit; review of the demonstrator; roles and scope confirmed | Scope and plan signed |
| **P1 Design and model** | 2 to 5 | Brand and interface design, clickable prototype, data and content model, hosting and staging set up; admin roles agreed | Design approved |
| **P2 Build** | 4 to 10 | In parallel streams: website, admin, culture and language modules; community engine and API; the mobile app with offline dictionary, outbox and push | Feature complete |
| **P3 Content, testing, pilot, training** | 9 to 12 | Content loaded and verified; functional, device, offline, accessibility, performance and security tests; pilot with 20 to 40 users from different districts; staff, moderator and clan-leader training | Acceptance signed |
| **P4 Launch** | 12 to 13 | Store submissions (Android first, then iOS; web app from day one), production go-live, launch communications | Go-live approved |
| **Hypercare** | 90 days after launch | Daily monitoring, rapid fixes, usage reviews at 30, 60 and 90 days, hand-over to a support plan | Hypercare report |

## How we work

**Two-week cycles** end with a demonstration on the staging site, so feedback goes straight into the next cycle. A **weekly 30-minute call** with a one-page status report, and a **monthly steering meeting** for leadership. One visible **backlog**, with change control: requests beyond the agreed scope are estimated and approved in writing before work starts. A feature is **done** only when built, tested on real devices, documented and accepted by the Foundation's coordinator.

## Content is a work stream

We **audit** what exists (registers, dictionaries, histories, photographs, recordings), agree a **minimum launch set** in week 2, prepare templates and CSV formats, **bulk-load** clans, dictionary entries and proverbs, and have the Foundation's panels **verify** them, with supervised interns helping with entry. The Foundation's verified content is never overwritten by updates.

## Quality and launch

Testing covers function, low- to high-end Android phones and iPhones, offline use, accessibility, performance on a throttled 4G connection, security (OWASP Top 10) and language (spelling, diacritics, bilingual consistency). No critical or high defect may remain open at go-live. Launch is a **soft launch** to the pilot group, then public, with a communications pack: press release, social assets, short how-to videos in English and Leb Lango, and posters with QR codes [TBC: launch event date and venue].

> **What makes three months possible:** the working demonstrator, a named Foundation coordinator in week 1, reviews within five working days, and a minimum launch content set agreed in week 2. If any of these slips, we say so early and re-plan together.
