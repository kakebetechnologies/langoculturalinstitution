# 1. Executive Summary

*A living, searchable, offline-ready home for Lango language, clans, history and heritage: one platform, delivered by a team that already built a working version.*

{{CLIENT}} is the keeper of a heritage that is older than any archive: the Leb Lango language, a clan system that runs to well over a hundred clans, the oral history of nine districts and Lira City, and the ceremonies, songs, crafts and proverbs that tie them together. Much of that knowledge still lives in the memory of elders, in family albums, in scattered documents and in WhatsApp groups. Every year some of it is lost. At the same time, a young, phone-first and diaspora-connected generation expects to find its culture where it already spends its time: on a screen in its pocket.

Kakebe Technologies Limited proposes to close that gap with **one digital platform made of three connected parts**:

| Part | What it is | Who it serves |
|---|---|---|
| **The public website** | A fast, bilingual, brand-led home for history, districts, clans, notable Langos, heritage, the Language Hub (dictionary, translator, proverbs, lessons), news, events, programmes and giving | Everyone: citizens, students, researchers, diaspora, partners, media |
| **The admin control panel** | A role-based workspace where the Foundation's own staff publish, review, translate and report, with no developer needed for day-to-day work | Editors, moderators, language experts, clan leaders, administrators |
| **The Lango Mobile App** | Android, iOS and installable web app with the offline dictionary, guided contribution forms (GPS, camera, voice), notifications and member recognition | Youth, clan members, contributors in the field, the diaspora |

All three share **one backend, one database and one review queue**. A word suggested on a phone in Aduku appears in the same moderation screen as a word suggested on a laptop in London; once an expert approves it, it is live in the dictionary and the translator on the website and in the app at the same moment.

## 1.1 Our recommendation

> **We recommend building the website and the mobile app together, on a single shared platform (Option B in Chapter 13).**

The reasons are practical, not fashionable:

1. **Reach.** The website is how the world finds the Foundation through search engines, shared links and press. The app is how the community uses it every day: offline, with the camera and microphone, with a push notification when a contribution is approved.
2. **Cost.** The app and the website use the same API, accounts, content and moderation. Building them together costs far less than building them one after the other, and it avoids two sources of truth.
3. **Capture.** The most valuable thing the Foundation can collect is knowledge that is not yet written down. That happens in villages, at ceremonies and in elders' homes, often without a data connection. Only an offline-first app can capture it there.
4. **Continuity.** Content, accounts and contributors are built once and reused for every future channel, whether that is SMS and USSD, a community forum or a radio programme.

## 1.2 This is not a concept: a working version already exists

Kakebe has already built and tested a complete working demonstrator of this platform for the Lango community (built under the working title *Lango Cultural Institution*). It is not a slide deck. You can log in to it today, and the scope in this proposal is measured against what it already does:

| What exists today | Measure |
|---|---|
| Public website | More than 30 page types: history, districts, clans, people, heritage, language, library, news, events, programmes, giving and more |
| Admin control panel | 30 managed content types plus bespoke screens for moderation, translations, notifications, users, audit log and backups |
| Mobile app | Around 50 screens on one codebase for Android, iOS and the installable web app; offline dictionary; offline contribution queue |
| Back end | A documented JSON API, a database of 32 tables, token-based sign-in, rate limiting and duplicate-safe uploads |
| Content rules | Every fact is sourced; nothing cultural is invented; the interface is built to receive the Foundation's own experts' words |

Starting from a working system changes the risk profile of the whole project. **Our first weeks are spent adapting, extending and filling the platform with the Foundation's authority, not discovering what a cultural platform is.** Appendix B shows the demonstrator screen by screen.

## 1.3 What the Foundation gains

- **A trusted public record** of the language, clans, districts, history and heritage, with sources shown and reviewers named.
- **A Language Hub** that teaches Leb Lango and makes it searchable online and offline, growing through community contributions that experts approve.
- **A community contribution engine** with tracking codes, GPS-tagged observations, photographs, audio and documents, reviewed in one queue.
- **A mobile presence** that works in low-connectivity areas and keeps the diaspora close.
- **Transparent giving and programmes:** pledges, programme progress and transparency figures that build donor confidence.
- **Independence:** an admin team trained to run the platform, full documentation, no lock-in, and ownership of the content and data.
- **A youth skills pipeline,** because the platform is built, maintained and populated in Lira City, with trainees from Kakebe's own programmes working under supervision.
