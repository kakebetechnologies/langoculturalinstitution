# 2. Understanding the Foundation's Needs

*What the platform must achieve, the challenges it must overcome, and the principles that guide every decision in this proposal.*

## 2.1 Context

The Lango people live in the Lango sub-region of northern Uganda, an area of nine districts (Lira, Apac, Oyam, Kole, Dokolo, Amolatar, Alebtong, Otuke and Kwania) and Lira City, home to more than two million people according to the 2024 census. Lira City is the commercial and educational centre of the region. The Lango language, Leb Lango, is spoken in homes, markets and on the radio; English and Kiswahili are used in offices and business.

{{CLIENT}} is the cultural institution that carries the identity of that community: its traditional leadership under the Won Nyaci, its clans, its ceremonies, its language and its history. A digital platform for such an institution is therefore not a marketing website. It is a **custodial** system, one that holds knowledge in trust, shows where each fact came from, and keeps authority over what is published firmly in the hands of the people the community recognises.

> **How we have written this proposal.** We describe the Foundation's needs as we understand them from public information and from the work we have already done on the demonstrator. Statements marked **[TBC]** are points to confirm with the Foundation at the discovery workshop. We would rather ask than assume.

## 2.2 Objectives we believe the platform should serve

| # | Objective | What success looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Preserve** | Language, clans, history and heritage are recorded in a durable, backed-up, searchable archive with sources |
| 2 | **Teach** | A young Lango, or a child abroad, can learn words, proverbs and greetings and hear how they are said |
| 3 | **Connect** | Clans, districts and the diaspora can find each other, take part and hear from the Foundation |
| 4 | **Represent** | The Foundation has an authoritative public voice and a trusted reference that media, schools and researchers can cite |
| 5 | **Sustain** | Programmes, events and giving are visible and accountable, supporting the Foundation's work |
| 6 | **Include** | The platform works for elders and youth, in town and village, online and offline |
| 7 | **Govern** | Staff can run it themselves, with clear roles, review and an audit trail |

These objectives are the yardstick we use throughout. Each feature in Chapters 4 to 6 exists to serve at least one of them, and Chapter 16 turns them into measurable indicators.

## 2.3 The challenges the platform must solve

| Challenge | Consequence if unaddressed | How the platform responds |
|---|---|---|
| **Knowledge is held in people, not on paper.** | When an elder passes away, a clan's oral history, songs and proverbs can go with them. | Guided contribution forms for oral history, proverbs, words, heritage sites and documents, with audio recording, photos and GPS, reviewed and archived. |
| **Language shift among youth.** | Fewer young people speak or write Leb Lango confidently. | Dictionary, English to Leb Lango translator, proverbs, greetings, phrasebook and lessons, usable offline on a phone. |
| **A dispersed community.** | Clans, nine districts and a large diaspora are hard to reach through one channel. | Website for reach and search, app for daily use, a diaspora corner, clan pages and a clan picker on every registration. |
| **Patchy and expensive data.** | A web-only platform excludes the people who most need it. | Offline-first app, versioned offline dictionary, stale-while-revalidate caching, queued uploads, lightweight pages. |
| **Concern about authenticity.** | Wrong or invented content would damage trust in the whole institution. | A strict content integrity policy: sources displayed, status labels, expert review, and nothing invented (Chapter 7 and Appendix C). |
| **Paper-based or ad hoc processes.** | Registrations, applications and event tickets are slow to process and hard to report on. | Online forms with tracking codes, a unified applications inbox, event registration with printable tickets. |
| **Limited IT capacity.** | A platform that needs a developer for every change will stall. | A no-code admin, role-based access, training, documentation and a support plan. |
| **Sensitive public matters.** | Leadership and other sensitive topics can be contested and must be handled with care. | Officeholders and titles are published exactly as the Foundation confirms, with an approval step; the platform takes no position. |
| **Funding and accountability.** | Donors and partners want to see where support goes. | Donation and pledge flow, programme progress bars and a transparency dashboard. |

## 2.4 Who the platform serves

| Audience | What they need | Where they meet the platform |
|---|---|---|
| **Youth and students** | Learn the language and history, find events, earn recognition | App, website |
| **Elders and knowledge holders** | Have their knowledge recorded and respected | Assisted contribution (a youth ambassador helps), admin review |
| **Clan leaders** | Maintain their clan's page and members, submit corrections | Clan self-service in the admin, website |
| **Diaspora** | Stay connected, learn, give, attend events | Website, app, diaspora corner |
| **Researchers, media and schools** | Citable, sourced material and open data | Website, library, open data API |
| **Partners and donors** | See programmes, progress and transparency | Website, programmes, transparency page |
| **Foundation staff** | Publish, review, report and communicate | Admin control panel |

## 2.5 Guiding principles

1. **Community authority.** The Foundation decides what is true and what is published. The platform records, shows sources and enforces review. It never invents culture.
2. **Offline first.** We design for the weakest connection, not the best one.
3. **Plain language.** Clear words, large touch targets, readable type and simple flows, so that a first-time smartphone user can take part.
4. **Own your data.** Content, accounts and contributions belong to the Foundation and can be exported at any time.
5. **Built to last.** Boring, well-understood technology that local developers can maintain on affordable hosting, with documentation and no lock-in.
6. **Respect for knowledge.** Consent, attribution and restricted-access protocols are built in from the start (Chapter 7).
