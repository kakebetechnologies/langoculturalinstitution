# 4. The Public Website

*A fast, bilingual, brand-led home for the Foundation's history, language, clans, heritage and community, designed to be read on a phone first.*

## 4.1 Experience and design

The website will carry the Foundation's own identity: its logo, colours, photography and tone. The demonstrator shows the approach: a contained editorial layout, a clear top bar with contact details, language switch and sign-in, and a full navigation with expandable sections on phones.

| Design principle | What it means for visitors |
|---|---|
| **Mobile first** | Pages are designed for a phone screen and then enlarged, because most visitors will arrive on a phone |
| **Bilingual** | A one-tap English / Leb Lango switch on every page; content and interface can each be translated by the Foundation's experts |
| **Brand-led** | The Foundation's logo, colours and photography, with the demonstrator's clean, editorial look as the starting point |
| **Readable** | Generous type size and contrast, WCAG 2.1 AA as the target, keyboard-friendly navigation |
| **Light** | Optimised images, sensible page weight and a lightweight mode for slow connections |
| **Findable** | Search-engine friendly addresses, structured data, a sitemap and share previews on every page |

## 4.3 The Language Hub

Language is the heart of the platform, and the demonstrator already shows how it should work.

- **Dictionary.** Search in either direction, filter by topic or letter, and browse A to Z. The search folds special characters, so typing *e* finds *ε* and *ng* finds *ŋ* on a plain keyboard.
- **Translator.** Translates between English and Leb Lango using the dictionary, not machine translation, and says so. Every published dictionary entry is available immediately.
- **Proverbs and greetings.** Published only when sourced or approved; none are pre-loaded unless the Foundation supplies them.
- **Lessons and phrasebook.** Short structured lessons and everyday phrases for learners.
- **Suggest a word or proverb.** Anyone can propose an entry. A duplicate check runs first; a language expert edits and approves; the entry then appears with a **Community** label until verified.
- **Verification labels.** Each entry shows whether it is *Verified by experts*, *Community* or *Unverified*, so users always know how much to trust it. The demonstrator already holds a starter dictionary of 2,829 entries, every one marked *unverified* until the Foundation's language experts review it.

## 4.4 History and heritage, done carefully

- **Sourced chapters.** History chapters are compiled from published sources, shown on the page, and flagged for the Foundation's historians to review before launch.
- **Timeline and glossary.** Key dates and terms are structured data, so they can be reused across the site and the app.
- **Heritage items** carry category, description, photographs, audio and, where relevant, a map location.
- **Corrections welcome.** Every page will offer a route for users to suggest a correction, which enters the same review queue.

## 4.5 Community participation

The website lets people do more than read.

- **One simple contribution form.** A visitor can submit a cultural observation, a natural heritage sighting, a word, a proverb, an oral history, a document or a heritage site. They can create an account on the spot, choose their clan, add GPS and upload photos, audio and files. They receive a **tracking code** to follow the submission.
- **Accounts, points, levels and badges** recognise contributors; a leaderboard celebrates the most active contributors.
- **Comments** on news and posts are moderated before they appear.
- **Clan picker** on every registration (account, contribution, scholarship, grant, volunteer, diaspora, event ticket, directory profile) with a "my clan is not listed" option that alerts administrators and builds the register over time.

![browser|Figure 4.5: The community contribution form with seven contribution types, optional account creation and a tracking code.](shots/site-contribute.jpg)

## 4.6 Diaspora corner

A dedicated diaspora section explains how Langos abroad can connect, give, volunteer and contribute, with a registration form that records country and clan. It gives the Foundation a measurable channel to an audience that is otherwise hard to reach.

## 4.7 Discoverability, analytics and sharing

- Search-engine friendly addresses, titles and descriptions on every page, an XML sitemap, and share previews (Open Graph) for WhatsApp, Facebook and X. Structured data for events and articles will be added in this engagement.
- Privacy-conscious analytics, added in this engagement, that show the Foundation which content people use and where they come from, without tracking individuals.
- A newsletter subscription with an exportable subscriber list.
